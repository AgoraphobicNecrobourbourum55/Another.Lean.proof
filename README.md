The six proofs, easy to hard:

X·X = I is a direct computation.
H′·H′ = 2·I is also a direct computation.
H′·X·H′ = 2·Z is the basis-change identity.
H′ doubles the squared norm for every integer vector, so this is a real algebraic proof instead of a computation.
No-cloning: no additive map can send every v to v⊗v. The proof compares the clone of (1,1) with the sum of the clones of (1,0) and (0,1).
Deutsch’s algorithm: for a constant f the amplitude on |0⟩ squares to 4 out of a total of 4, and for a balanced f it is exactly 0.
